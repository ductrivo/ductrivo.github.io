### How has the Dent de Crolles maintained its aerial features despite underground erosion?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/96462248-38b5-478d-9903-e28fd4b0d1c7/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="How has the Dent de Crolles maintained its aerial features despite underground erosion?"
    caption="How has the Dent de Crolles maintained its aerial features despite underground erosion?" %}


The natural site of Dent de Crolles, located in France, features well-preserved rock formations despite underground erosion. Although several tens of kilometers of underground caves have been created by water infiltration, the mountain is not yet so weakened that there is currently a cause for concern. Dent de Crolles is a limestone formation that formed approximately 200 million years ago, and its interior contains complex underground caves created by water. While the presence of these caves could potentially weaken the mountain, geologists have observed that they have not yet had a negative impact on the stability of the rock formations. This may be due to several factors, such as the absence of heavy rainfall or earthquakes in the region, which could accelerate underground erosion and weaken the mountain. In any case, Dent de Crolles remains a remarkable natural site to visit, offering spectacular views of the surrounding area.
