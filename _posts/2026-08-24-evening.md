### Here, it works, Here, Isère


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/f634d917-8e35-4937-9ad8-8242dd02de96/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Here, it works, Here, Isère"
    caption="Here, it works, Here, Isère" %}


In the current French department of Isère, evidence of a remarkable invention dating over a thousand years ago can be found: the "galoche". This wooden shoe was widely used by the local population during the Gallo-Roman period. The galoche consisted of a piece of hard wood, typically pine or oak, which served as a sole and a leather strap to hold the wood on the shoe. This allowed people to move around in wet areas without worrying about mud and swamps. Galoches were discovered during archaeological excavations, notably in Saint-Marcel-lès-Valence, where they were found alongside other objects such as pottery and Gallo-Roman coins. Today, the galoches are an important symbol of local history and are exhibited in several museums in the region.

### 'LSD is active at minuscule doses': between bad trips and hopes for psychiatry, the ambiguous status of LSD

However, LSD has also been used in some forms of psychiatric therapy, particularly for treating schizophrenia and drug-resistant depression. Research has shown that the use of LSD can help to alleviate the symptoms of these diseases by allowing patients to explore their deep emotions and shape their behavior.

The legal status of LSD is ambiguous; it is classified as an illegal substance in most countries, but some organizations have called for its decriminalization for therapeutic use.
