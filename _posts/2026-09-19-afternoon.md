### Henri Fabre, the grandfather of the hydroplane


Henri Fabre, considered the pioneer of naval aviation, was born in 1882 in Marseille. His father was from Marseille, and his mother was from Dauphiné. He began to be interested in aviation at the age of 16, after reading the work of the French engineer Clément Ader.

In 1908, Fabre built his first aircraft, the "Canard," which is powered by a steam engine. He made his first flight in October of that year. In 1910, he designed and built the hydroplane "Sesostris I," which is equipped with a gasoline engine and can take off and land on water. This model is considered the first successful hydroplane in history.

In 1912, Fabre founded his own company, the Société des Avions Henri Fabre, which produced military aircraft for the French army during World War I. After the war, he continued to work on hydroplanes and designed several successful models, including the "Morse-Fabre" in 1920.

Henri Fabre died in 1984 at the age of 102. He left a significant legacy in the field of aviation, particularly as a pioneer of naval aviation and as an innovative engineer.
