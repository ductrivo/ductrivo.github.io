### What caused the tragic flood in Grenoble in 1219?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/2d8dd83f-3ee3-4b5e-9b46-4d19547393cf/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="What caused the tragic flood in Grenoble in 1219?"
    caption="What caused the tragic flood in Grenoble in 1219?" %}


The tragic flood in Grenoble in 1219 occurred on September 14th around midnight, when a vast wave of water, mud, and rock flooded the waters of the Drac River and overflowed onto the Grenoble plain. This is likely the worst flood the city has ever experienced. The cause of this disaster is still debated, but it is generally believed to be related to torrential rainfall and a landslide in the Alps. The consequences of the flood were disastrous: the city was almost completely destroyed, and the number of casualties is estimated to be in the thousands. This disaster marked a significant turning point in the history of Grenoble, which had to be rebuilt after this tragedy.

The village of Notre-Dame-de-l'Osier gets its name from a supernatural event that occurred in the 17th century, which has since been reported and celebrated by a shrine. A Protestant farmer witnessed two unexplained events: the miraculous healing of a Catholic woman who had been swept away by the waters of the Isère River, and the apparition of the Virgin Mary to a shepherd.
