### Choucroute: From Korea to the United States, a traditional Alsatian dish with international variations.


{% include figure.html
    src="https://france3-regions.franceinfo.fr/image/ix9DmXK1cxc1P9nMiste3H4q0RY/930x620/filters:format(webp)/regions/2026/10/29/6ae3078855870958700612.jpg"
    alt="Choucroute: From Korea to the United States, a traditional Alsatian dish with international variations."
    caption="Choucroute: From Korea to the United States, a traditional Alsatian dish with international variations." %}


Sauerkraut is a traditional dish originating in Alsace, France, but which has spread internationally, from Korea to the United States. It consists of cabbage, sausages, bacon, and potatoes. In France, 38,000 tonnes of sauerkraut are produced each year, which ranks among the top 5 favorite dishes in the country. Despite its popularity, it is interesting to know its history.

Sauerkraut has its roots in the history of Alsace, a border region with Germany. Initially, it was a way to preserve food during long winter periods using salt and spices. The sausages were imported from Bavaria, and the cabbage was grown locally.

Over the centuries, sauerkraut has evolved and gained new variations. In the United States, for example, it is often prepared with salami and salted potatoes. In Korea, Chinese cabbage is used instead of traditional white cabbage.

Today, sauerkraut remains a symbol of Alsatian cuisine, but its international popularity reflects its spread and adaptation to local tastes. It has also become a symbol of Alsatian cultural identity, often associated with festive traditions and local celebrations.
