### At the heart of the TER high-speed rail project that went off course between Rouen and Caen


On May 15th, a regional express train (TER) derailed between the cities of Rouen and Caen in France. The accident occurred near the A13 motorway, around 6:40 AM local time. Emergency services were immediately mobilized at the scene to assist passengers and crew members.

Authorities confirmed that 25 people were injured in the accident, with two seriously injured. The injured were transported to local hospitals to receive necessary care. The transport service also announced that it had put in place an emergency plan to ensure the continuity of rail service in the region.

Investigators began working at the accident site to determine the exact cause of the derailment. Initial information suggests that the train may have exceeded the speed limit before derailing. The investigation is ongoing, and results are expected in the coming weeks.

French Transport Minister Jean-Baptiste Djebbari stated that the accident was a priority for the government and that he had ordered a full investigation to determine the circumstances of the accident. He also confirmed that the rail service would be restored in the region as soon as possible.

The TER accident between Rouen and Caen has sparked a strong reaction from the media and passengers, who have expressed their concern for the victims of the accident. Authorities have called for caution and adherence to railway safety rules to prevent such accidents in the future.
