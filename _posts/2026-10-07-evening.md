{% include figure.html

    caption="Why is the 'Devil's Stone' of Artas called that demonic name?" %}


The demonic name of the "Devil's Stone" of Artas is linked to its origin and its strange position in the forest of North Isère, France. It is a massive rock that was moved during the last glacial period, several tens of thousands of years ago. The stone is lost in the forest, and its irregular shape and strange location have given rise to local legends that it was moved by the devil or is a satanic symbol. However, it is a natural phenomenon resulting from glaciation, not devilry. The elements of the answer are the archaeological and geological discoveries that have allowed us to understand the origin of the "Devil's Stone" of Artas.

### Why does the fountain in Nivolas-Vermelle bear the name Jean-Jacques Rousseau?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/1fbd24a4-ae83-4d46-9573-3b98f3204a30/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Why does the fountain in Nivolas-Vermelle bear the name Jean-Jacques Rousseau?"
    caption="Why does the fountain in Nivolas-Vermelle bear the name Jean-Jacques Rousseau?" %}


The fountain in Nivolas-Vermelle is named after Jean-Jacques Rousseau because of his long stay in the north of the Dauphiné region, near Bourgoin-Jallieu, for almost two years. The philosopher of the Enlightenment spent much of his time exploring the forests and paths of the region. Although Rousseau did not live in immediate proximity to the fountain, his presence in the area contributed to associating his name with this site.
