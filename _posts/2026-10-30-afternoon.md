### Why is the dungeon of the old Pariset castle, in the Vercors, called the 'Tour-sans-Venin' (Tower without Poison)?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/a6ef7dc1-952f-45e5-94f8-9bf946e574b9/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Why is the dungeon of the old Pariset castle, in the Vercors, called the 'Tour-sans-Venin' (Tower without Poison)?"
    caption="Why is the dungeon of the old Pariset castle, in the Vercors, called the 'Tour-sans-Venin' (Tower without Poison)?" %}


The tower of the old Pariset castle, located in the Vercors, is known as the "Tour-sans-Venin" (Tower Without Venom). Built in the Middle Ages, this tower stands on a hill and is subject to various legends and historical theories.

One of the most famous stories says that the tower was named in honor of the Egyptian goddess Sekhmet, who was invoked to protect the castle from venomous snakes. Another theory suggests that Emperor Charles Martel or his son Charlemagne gave the tower its name in reference to a local knight who had returned from the Crusades, who was saved from a venomous snake by a local virgin.

A third legend says that the famous Roland, one of Charlemagne's twelve knights, was also saved from a venomous snake in the area and gave his name to the tower in gratitude.

Despite the many legends, there is no solid evidence to confirm either of the historical theories. The "Tour-sans-Venin" remains a mysterious and romantic symbol in the landscape of the Vercors.
