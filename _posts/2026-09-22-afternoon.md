### Why does the sculpture that sits atop the Méaudre fountain depict a cherub with a fish?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/949d522d-f0c1-4240-901b-c9215e8b7aac/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Why does the sculpture that sits atop the Méaudre fountain depict a cherub with a fish?"
    caption="Why does the sculpture that sits atop the Méaudre fountain depict a cherub with a fish?" %}


The cherub with a fish, which adorns the fountain in Méaudre, is an artistic symbol dating back to 1892. This sculpture marks the end of a long legal battle and more than thirty years of struggles for the town hall of Méaudre and its residents.

In 1857, the town of Méaudre had commissioned a fountain from a local sculptor, but the project was never completed. In 1892, the town hall decided to reclaim the funds originally allocated for the fountain and entrust its creation to another sculptor, Auguste-Joseph Dubois.

However, the sculptor had already agreed to create a statue for the neighboring town of Saint-Quentin, which led to financial and legal difficulties for the town hall of Méaudre. The residents had to mobilize to defend their town and demand the return of the funds allocated to the fountain.

After several years of legal proceedings, the residents of Méaudre were finally able to obtain the return of the funds and the creation of the fountain with the cherub and fish sculpture. This work thus symbolizes not only art, but also the resilience and spirit of self-determination of the residents of Méaudre.
