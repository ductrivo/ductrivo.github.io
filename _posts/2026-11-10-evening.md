The Balcons du Dauphiné, a mountainous region in eastern France, are known for having housed around two fortified houses per commune in the Middle Ages. These fortifications were built to protect the borders of the Dauphiné, an ancient feudal state that existed at the time. In total, around a hundred fortified houses were erected in the region during this historical period. These defensive structures were intended to protect the inhabitants from external attacks and to maintain the security of the region. ### From super secretary to super resistor


Anne-Marie Lerme Mingat, a secretary who became an exceptional resistance fighter. She transformed her talent for writing with a simple typewriter into a vital tool for saving lives during World War II.

Born on January 28, 1907, in Paris, Anne-Marie Lerme Mingat began her professional career as a secretary in a construction company. However, when the war broke out in 1939, she decided to put her skills to use for France and joined the Free French Forces.

As a member of the "Buckmaster" resistance network, Anne-Marie Lerme Mingat played a crucial role in transmitting sensitive information between London and the headquarters of the Allied forces in France. She also used her typewriter to produce false documents, thus contributing to the defeat of the Germans.

In 1943, Anne-Marie Lerme Mingat was arrested by the Germans and imprisoned for several months. Despite the torture she endured, she revealed no information about her resistance network. She was finally released in 1945, after the victory of the Allies.

After the war, Anne-Marie Lerme Mingat returned to her civilian life and died on January 28, 1976, in Paris. Her courage and dedication to France were recognized by the French authorities, who awarded her several honorary distinctions. She is considered a war heroine and an example of patriotic commitment.
