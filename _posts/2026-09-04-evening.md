### Why does Chartreuse wood have a Protected Designation of Origin (PDO)?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/bb0c0811-1cd0-4ba8-813d-78b0083cf5fb/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Why does Chartreuse wood have a Protected Designation of Origin (PDO)?"
    caption="Why does Chartreuse wood have a Protected Designation of Origin (PDO)?" %}


The Chartreuse forest is a forest located in the French Alps, near Grenoble, which obtained Protected Designation of Origin (PDO) status in 2018. This PDO protects the quality and authenticity of Chartreuse wood, which is used to produce Chartreuse liqueur, a famous green liqueur with aromatic herb flavors.

The Chartreuse forest covers more than 50,000 hectares and is composed of many tree species, including Scots pine, beech, and pedunculate oak. The trees are cultivated using traditional and environmentally friendly methods, which have been in place for several centuries by the Carthusian monks, who founded their first abbey in the forest in the Middle Ages.

Chartreuse liqueur is produced by two distilleries, Maison des Pères Chartreux and Maison Carthusian Distilleries, which use the forest's trees to extract the necessary aromatic components for its production. The production methods are strictly controlled and respect ancient traditions, which give Chartreuse liqueur its unique flavor and PDO status.

Therefore, the Chartreuse forest is a historic and cultural forest, which has given rise to one of the world's most famous liqueurs. Its protection by the Protected Designation of Origin guarantees its quality and authenticity for future generations.
