### Tens of thousands of protesters in the streets across France against 'the right to kill' by law enforcement.


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/9feb8cc3-6e00-4013-855a-b7630297cc32/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Tens of thousands of protesters in the streets across France against 'the right to kill' by law enforcement."
    caption="Tens of thousands of protesters in the streets across France against 'the right to kill' by law enforcement." %}


In France, tens of thousands of protesters took to the streets this weekend to protest against the proposed law on the presumption of lawful use of force by law enforcement. More than 20,000 people gathered in Paris, Marseille, Lyon, and Toulouse. Among them were a large number of supporters of the Left Party (LFI). The protesters believe that this proposed law amounts to a "license to kill" for law enforcement. They are concerned that it would allow police officers to use their firearms without consequences, which could lead to an increase in police violence. The demonstrations were relatively peaceful, but there were some incidents of vandalism and clashes with the police.

    src="https://france3-regions.franceinfo.fr/image/DSvwrm2XuQcqtTycg7Dn0udAjqU/930x620/filters:format(webp)/regions/2026/09/20/6ab0114c4b888035370298.jpg"

This law is causing widespread controversy among the French population, particularly in Lyon, Clermont-Ferrand, Saint-Étienne, and Grenoble, where protests have taken place against the law on the presumption of the legitimate use of firearms. The proposed law was approved by parliament on July 7 and provides that law enforcement could use firearms without having to prove that they were necessary in each individual case.
