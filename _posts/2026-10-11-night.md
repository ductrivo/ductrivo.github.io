### OAT, spread, deficit… Debt for dummies


The deficit is the difference between a country's or public administration's revenues and expenditures over a given period. When expenditures exceed revenues, this is referred to as a budget deficit.

Debt is the total of financial obligations incurred by a country or public administration. It can be divided into two categories: public debt and private debt. Public debt consists of debts incurred by national, regional, or local governments. Private debt, on the other hand, consists of debts incurred by businesses and individuals.

The interest rate is the compensation that borrowers must pay to lenders for the use of their money. The interest rate can vary depending on the perceived risk by investors, who may be more or less likely to lose their money if the lender does not fulfill its financial obligations.

The spread is the difference between the interest rate of one borrower and that of another borrower considered as a benchmark. For example, if a country has an interest rate of 5% and the United States has an interest rate of 3%, the spread between these two countries is 2%.

Finally, the OAT (Obligation Assimilée au Trésor) is a French Treasury bond issued on the bond market. It is considered a benchmark instrument for French public debt and is used by central banks for their monetary operations.
