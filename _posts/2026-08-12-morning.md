### Glasses, schedules, and locations: our tips for enjoying the solar eclipse


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/db075872-40c4-40f1-80eb-6c119c574d98/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Glasses, schedules, and locations: our tips for enjoying the solar eclipse"
    caption="Lunettes, horaires, lieux adaptés : nos conseils pour profiter de l'éclipse solaire" %}


On August 12, 2026, a solar eclipse will be visible in mainland France. It will be partial but very pronounced in the southwest, with the maximum occurring just before sunset. Here are the tips for enjoying this astronomical event:

1. Eye protection: It is important to use specific eye protection to view the solar eclipse, as sunlight can seriously damage the eyes. The eye protection must be certified ISO 12312-2 or CE.
2. Times: The solar eclipse will begin around 6:45 PM and last for about two hours. The maximum will be reached just before sunset, around 8:15 PM.
3. Suitable locations: The most suitable locations for observing the eclipse are in the southwest of France, particularly in Aquitaine and Midi-Pyrénées. It is recommended to choose an open location with a view to the west to fully enjoy the event.
4. Precautions: It is important to take precautions when observing the solar eclipse, particularly by avoiding looking directly at the Sun before or after the eclipse, and by not leaving the eye protection in the sunlight for too long.
5. Astronomical event: The solar eclipse is an exceptional natural event that occurs when the Earth is between the Sun and the Moon, allowing a part of the Sun's surface to be obscured by the Moon. This event is rare, as it occurs only a few times per year and only in certain parts of the world.
