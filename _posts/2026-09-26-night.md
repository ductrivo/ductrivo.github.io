### Papal visit to France: after Paris and Lourdes, why does the Pope go to Metz?


Pope Leo XIV makes a visit to France in 1864. After stops in Paris and Lourdes, he also visits Metz. This city was chosen for several reasons. Firstly, it is located in the historical region of Lorraine, which had recently been annexed by France in 1859. The Pope thus wishes to show his support for France and its territorial policy.

Secondly, Metz was a strategically and militarily important city, having been the headquarters of one of the largest Prussian garrisons in France until the Franco-Prussian War of 1870. The Pope can thus show his support for France in its struggle against foreign powers.

Finally, Metz is also a historically important city for Catholicism in France. It has been the seat of an episcopate since the 5th century and has hosted several important councils of the Catholic Church. The Pope can thus show his support for Catholic faith in France and strengthen the ties between the Church and French believers.

In summary, Pope Leo XIV visits Metz to show his support for France in its territorial policy, in its struggle against foreign powers, and in its Catholic faith.
