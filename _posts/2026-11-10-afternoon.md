### Eternal pollutants: explorer Rémi Camus returns to the Drôme to present the results of his Rhône samples


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/f7787cc0-b839-46c5-a0c5-905f196e7f57/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Eternal pollutants: explorer Rémi Camus returns to the Drôme to present the results of his Rhône samples"
    caption="Eternal pollutants: explorer Rémi Camus returns to the Drôme to present the results of his Rhône samples" %}


Researcher Rémi Camus presented the results of his samples taken from the Rhône River in Drôme. After a year of analysis, he found the presence of persistent organic and artificial pollutants (PFAS) in the river water. He made stops in Valence and Montélimar to share his local findings. PFAS are toxic chemical substances that are resistant to climate change and are widely present in the environment. They can have harmful effects on human and animal health, as well as on aquatic ecosystems. The sources of these pollutants are diverse, including industrial and military activities, as well as everyday products such as oil-resistant coatings and cleaning products. Camus's study highlights the importance of investigating the presence of these pollutants in surface waters, particularly due to their persistence and toxicity.
