### El Niño: this climate phenomenon that worries scientists


El Niño is a marine weather phenomenon that occurs periodically in the Pacific Ocean, thus affecting the global climate. It is characterized by an increase in the surface water temperature and a change in ocean currents in the equatorial part of this ocean.

This phenomenon occurs when the warm waters of the central part of the Pacific Ocean move towards the east, towards South America, disrupting the global climate system. The effects of El Niño can vary and include abnormal rainfall in certain regions, high or abnormal temperatures, more or less frequent tropical cyclones, as well as changes in wildlife and flora.

Scientists observe that El Niño events occur every 2 to 7 years, with durations ranging from a few months to several years. The effects of El Niño can have negative consequences on agriculture, public health and the environment, particularly in terms of crop yields, epidemics and endangered species.

Scientists are currently studying the causes and effects of El Niño in order to better understand its impact on the global climate and to predict future events. They use numerical weather forecasting models, satellite data and in-situ observations to track the evolution of this phenomenon.

In conclusion, El Niño is a marine climate phenomenon that occurs periodically in the Pacific Ocean and affects the global climate. It can have negative effects on agriculture, public health and the environment, but scientists are currently studying its causes and effects in order to better understand its impact and predict future events.
