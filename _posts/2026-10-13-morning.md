### 'She was only 12 years old': Climate change, a new accelerator of child marriage


Climate change is a factor that contributes to the increase in the phenomenon of child marriage. According to a study published in the journal Nature Climate Change, extreme weather conditions can lead to increased poverty and natural disasters, which can lead to early marriages among children.

The research was conducted in 10 developing countries, including India, Pakistan, and Senegal. Researchers found that when weather conditions deteriorate, families are often forced to resort to survival strategies, such as migration or child labor. These situations can lead to an increase in the rate of child marriage, as parents see it as a way to provide an additional source of income for their family.

The research also showed that early marriage has negative consequences for the health and education of children. Young girls who are married are more likely to be victims of domestic violence, while boys are more likely to be unable to complete their education.

Researchers also emphasized the importance of taking climate change into account in policies to combat child marriage. They recommended measures such as the implementation of programs to support families, the promotion of education, and the protection of children's rights.

In conclusion, climate change can have a negative impact on child marriage in some developing countries. Policies must therefore take this factor into account in order to combat this phenomenon and protect the rights of children.
