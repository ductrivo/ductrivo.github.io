### Why is the Isère department the birthplace of mountain flying?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/95b6a880-9374-424f-81f6-9a0c2f309625/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Why is the Isère department the birthplace of mountain flying?"
    caption="Why is the Isère department the birthplace of mountain flying?" %}


The Isère department is known as the birthplace of mountain flying due to its remarkable history in this field. This aerial practice, which was initially reserved for an elite, was pioneered and developed locally by a pioneer, a man known as "the bird."

Specifically, Georges Frégier, born in 1892 in Grenoble, is considered the first to have successfully performed mountain flights. In 1913, he made a 400-meter flight at an altitude of 2,500 meters on the slopes of Mont Blanc, marking the beginning of a new era in mountain aviation.

Frégier continued to explore and improve the practice of mountain flying for several years. In 1920, he set a world record for distance with a flight of 35 kilometers between Grenoble and Saint-Marcellin. His work contributed to popularizing this aerial discipline and to the creation of a mountain flying school in Grenoble in 1924.

Since then, the Isère department has been recognized as a world center for mountain flying, attracting pilots from all over the world to train and participate in competitions. The French Alps offer a variety of ideal conditions for this aerial discipline, with their steep slopes and high altitude.

In conclusion, the Isère department is the birthplace of mountain flying thanks to the contribution of Georges Frégier, who pioneered and developed this practice in the French Alps at the beginning of the 20th century.
