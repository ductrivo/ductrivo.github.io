### Drôme - Ardèche. 'Talking about death doesn't make it come any faster': they support people at the end of their lives.


{% include figure.html
    src="https://cdn-s-www.ledauphine.com/images/2DDA491C-3E70-4FED-9170-A3DAC7708AAF/NW_listB/parler-de-la-mort-ne-la-fait-pas-venir-plus-vite-ils-soutiennent-les-personnes-en-fin-de-vie-1793453407.jpg"
    alt="Drôme - Ardèche. 'Talking about death doesn't make it come any faster': they support people at the end of their lives."
    caption="Drôme - Ardèche. 'Talking about death doesn't make it come any faster': they support people at the end of their lives." %}


The association Jusqu'à la mort accompagner la vie (Jalmalv) has been active in the departments of Drôme and Ardèche since 1989. Its main objective is to provide support to people in their final stages of life and their loved ones. In addition to providing practical services, the association also seeks to contribute to changing attitudes towards illness, death, and bereavement.

Sandrine Perriquet, the local branch president, explained that their mission is to support people in their final stages of life in their personal process and to help them live their last moments with dignity and peace. They also provide emotional support to the loved ones of the deceased, as well as to caregivers who deal with death daily.

The Jalmalv association regularly organizes events and activities to raise awareness about end-of-life issues, such as conferences, workshops, and meetings with healthcare professionals. They also work in collaboration with other organizations to promote a more open and accepting culture around death.

Finally, Sandrine Perriquet emphasized the importance of talking openly and honestly about death, as it does not hasten its arrival, but can help to make the process more bearable for people in their final stages of life and their loved ones.

### The mysterious wallet, recovered from the ice of Mont Blanc, reveals its secrets… 40 years later


The mysterious wallet discovered in the ice of Mont Blanc in 1949 finally revealed its secrets 40 years later. The object, which had been found by Swiss climbers, contained documents and photographs related to a gold heist that took place during World War II.

The wallet belonged to a certain Gustav Wouters, a Belgian jewelry dealer who had been arrested in 1946 for selling stolen diamonds to the Nazis. He was suspected of having participated in the gold heist, but he was released without trial because there was no evidence.

The documents found in the wallet confirmed that Wouters had indeed participated in the gold heist, which took place in Brussels in 1943. The loot consisted of 200 kilos of gold and diamonds, and it had been stolen by a group of Belgian and Dutch dealers.

The Swiss climbers had found the wallet in 1949 when they were climbing Mont Blanc for scientific research. They had left it in a Swiss museum, where it remained until 2019, when it was put up for auction.

The wallet was finally sold for over 3 million euros to a private collector. The Belgian authorities requested its return, but their request was rejected by the Swiss court.
