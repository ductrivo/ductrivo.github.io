### Champions League: How will the €2.4 billion promised by UEFA to clubs be distributed?


The UEFA Champions League prize distribution system has been modified for the 2021-2022 season. Participating clubs will receive a total of €2.4 billion, an increase of 30% compared to the previous season.

The funds will be distributed in several categories:

1. The "participation fee" is awarded to clubs participating in the group stage and amounts to €15.25 million per team. Clubs eliminated before the round of 16 receive a lower amount.
2. "Win bonuses" are awarded to clubs that win their respective matches and amount to €3.05 million per win in the group stage and up to €4.75 million in the final phase.
3. "Final phase participation bonuses" are awarded to clubs that reach the round of 16, quarter-finals, semi-finals, and final. These bonuses amount to €9.5 million for the round of 16, €10.5 million for the quarter-finals, €12 million for the semi-finals, and €15 million for the final.
4. "UEFA Super Cup participation bonuses" are awarded to clubs that win the Champions League or the Europa League. This bonus amounts to €5.5 million.
5. "FIFA Club World Cup participation bonuses" are awarded to clubs that win the Champions League. This bonus amounts to €6 million.

In addition to these bonuses, clubs can also receive additional funds based on their performance in the competition, such as the UEFA coefficient and bonuses for televised matches.

### 'Slaughtering the Balkans': The funeral that could cost Serbia Europe


The title "Butcher of the Balkans" refers to the assassination of King Alexander I of Yugoslavia in October 1934, during an official visit to Marseille in France. The perpetrator of the assassination was a Serbian nationalist, Vlada Čeča, who aimed to provoke a war between Serbia and Yugoslavia.

The assassination had serious consequences for all of Europe. The death of King Alexander I led to a political crisis in Yugoslavia, with growing tensions between the different ethnic groups in the country. The Serbs accused the Croats and Slovenes of complicity in the assassination, which led to inter-ethnic violence.

The political crisis in Yugoslavia also had repercussions on European foreign policy. France and the United Kingdom attempted to mediate between the different parties, but their intervention could not prevent the civil war that broke out in Yugoslavia in 1941.

The civil war led to the disintegration of Yugoslavia and the creation of several independent states, including Serbia. Serbia was one of the main actors in the war, with active participation in the fighting against the Allied forces.

The assassination of King Alexander I therefore cost Europe dearly, as it caused a political and military crisis that lasted for several years and led to the disintegration of Yugoslavia.
