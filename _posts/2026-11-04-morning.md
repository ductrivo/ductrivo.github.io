### Why did wine production, which was very important in Isère in the 19th century, almost cease?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/5cd7db2f-4703-4ec7-beb4-348fc3ca777f/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Why did wine production, which was very important in Isère in the 19th century, almost cease?"
    caption="Why did wine production, which was very important in Isère in the 19th century, almost cease?" %}


Wine production in Isère, which was significant in the 19th century, has almost ceased due to several reasons. Until the early 1860s, the department was one of the main wine-producing regions in France. However, a wave of a vine disease known as phylloxera devastated the Isère vineyards starting in the 1860s. This disease is caused by a parasitic insect that attacks the roots of vines, causing their death. In addition to the disease, changes in habits also contributed to the decline in wine production in Isère. Consumers began to prefer wines produced in other regions, such as Bordeaux and Champagne, which led to a decrease in demand for Isère wine. Furthermore, industrialization and urbanization also contributed to the disappearance of the Isère vineyards due to the conversion of agricultural land into industrial or residential areas. Today, only a few vineyards remain in the department, but they do not represent a significant part of the national wine production.
