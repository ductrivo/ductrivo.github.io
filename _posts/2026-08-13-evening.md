### 'An absolutely unacceptable visit': Why Vladimir Putin's visit to the Kuril Islands is angering Japan


Vladimir Putin's visit to the Kuril Islands, a territory claimed by Japan since World War II, has provoked a strong reaction from the Japanese government. The Kuril Islands, also known as the Sousou-Ryū Islands in Japanese, were occupied by the Soviet Union in 1945 and annexed by Russia in 1947. Japan still considers these islands to be part of its national territory, which has led to diplomatic tensions between the two countries. Putin's visit to the Kuril Islands is seen by Japan as a violation of its sovereignty over the territory and an affront to its territorial claims. The Japanese government has condemned the visit, stating that it exacerbates tensions between the two countries and is not conducive to regional stability. Diplomatic relations between Japan and Russia are already strained due to several territorial and economic issues, including the Sousou-Ryū Islands, as well as disputes over natural resources in the Sea of Japan and economic sanctions imposed on Russia by the European Union.
