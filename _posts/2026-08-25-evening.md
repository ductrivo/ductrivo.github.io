### Heat in schools: 'We need to do things in the right order,' advises Vincent Revol of ALEC


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/ca54fb36-13e9-4abb-8122-9ce518583526/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Heat in schools: 'We need to do things in the right order,' advises Vincent Revol of ALEC"
    caption="Heat in schools: 'We need to do things in the right order,' advises Vincent Revol of ALEC" %}


Climate change is causing an increase in temperatures in schools, which poses challenges for adapting school facilities. Vincent Revol, from the ALEC (Local Energy and Climate Agency), proposes a phased approach, starting with adapting schools to heat.

Before the next school year, it is important that schools are equipped to manage the effects of climate change. In the Isère department, renovation work is underway to improve the insulation and ventilation of schools.

The measures being taken include the installation of natural ventilation systems, the use of insulating materials in walls and roofs, as well as the improvement of heating and air conditioning systems. These renovations aim to reduce energy consumption and reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

In addition to renovations, it is also important that teachers and students are informed about the measures to take to manage heat in schools. This includes the use of lightweight clothing, taking measures to reduce energy consumption during peak heat hours, and encouraging regular hydration to prevent dehydration.

Finally, schools can also take measures to reduce their carbon footprint by using renewable energy sources, such as wind or solar power, and by promoting sustainable mobility, such as cycling or walking.

In summary, to manage heat in schools, it is important to renovate school facilities to improve their insulation and ventilation, to inform teachers and students about the measures to take to manage heat, and to take measures to reduce the schools' carbon footprint.

The Laval-en-Belledonne hydroelectric power plant, located in the French Alps, was built inside a narrow gorge that surrounds a stream. ### Drôme. Who was Gustave André, who gave his name to several schools in the department?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://cdn-s-www.ledauphine.com/images/2893EFCC-5DC1-464B-8D85-D76D40871D8A/NW_listB/qui-etait-gustave-andre-qui-a-donne-son-nom-a-plusieurs-ecoles-du-departement-1787576489.jpg"
    alt="Drôme. Who was Gustave André, who gave his name to several schools in the department?"
    caption="Drôme. Who was Gustave André, who gave his name to several schools in the department?" %}


Gustave André was a teacher born in Chabeuil (Drôme) who gave his name to several schools in the department. August 29th will mark the 82nd anniversary of his death, when he was executed by the Germans for his involvement in the French Resistance during World War II.

Born in 1896, Gustave André began his career as a teacher in Chabrillan, where he was also mayor from 1935 to 1942. In 1940, he joined the Resistance and actively participated in actions against German occupation.

In 1943, he was arrested by the Germans and imprisoned in Grenoble. Despite several attempts at negotiation for his release, he was sentenced to death and executed on August 29, 1940.

Gustave André is considered a hero of the Resistance in Drôme, and his actions have been recognized through tributes and schools named after him. His story remains a source of inspiration for those who fight against oppression and resist occupation.
