### Why is there a statue of Liberty in Roybon on the Chambaran plateau?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/d25abed0-4aa0-4bb3-a14a-b15e022809cf/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Why is there a statue of Liberty in Roybon on the Chambaran plateau?"
    caption="Why is there a statue of Liberty in Roybon on the Chambaran plateau?" %}


The Statue of Liberty on display in Roybon on the Chambaran plateau is actually a miniaturized replica of the original model designed by sculptor Auguste Bartholdi, who is famous for creating the Statue of Liberty installed in New York Bay since 1886. This replica was originally created to be exhibited in Paris in 1879 during a World's Fair, but it was not ultimately installed there. In 1880, the statue was gifted to the town of Roybon by a private French collector, where it is still visible today.

{% include figure.html
    src="https://cdn-s-www.ledauphine.com/images/52C5BD67-CFD2-4D74-A621-7E4A80BB42E5/NW_listB/avec-la-refonte-des-championnats-comment-vont-fonctionner-les-promotions-et-relegations-cette-saison-1788985969.jpg"
    alt="Rugby - Amateur. With the restructuring of the championships, how will promotions and relegations work this season?"
    caption="Rugby - Amateur. With the restructuring of the championships, how will promotions and relegations work this season?" %}


The French federal rugby league is undergoing a reorganization of the championships for the 2027-2028 season. Promotions and relegations will be managed according to a new system.

The French Rugby Federation has decided to align the number of divisions with that used in other sports in France, which will go from 16 to 12. The first division will remain Pro D2, but the following divisions will be renamed: National 1 will become National A, National 2 will become National B, and so on until the 12th division.

Promotions and relegations will take place between each division, with an exception for Pro D2, which will no longer be directly linked to the Ligue Nationale de Rugby (LNR). Instead, the two best teams from Pro D2 will play a playoff against the last team in the LNR.

This reorganization aims to improve the competitiveness of the championships and to give more visibility to federal rugby clubs in France. The practical details of this new structure will be communicated by the FFR in the coming months.
