### Françoise Gignoux of Grenoble, who has marked the snow as much as the asphalt, is a champion skier and driver.


Françoise Gignoux, born in Grenoble, left her mark on both snow and asphalt, being an outstanding champion in winter sports like alpine skiing and summer sports like aviation. She is unique among top athletes for having achieved two exceptional careers.

Born in 1930, Françoise Gignoux began her skiing career at the age of 15. She won several national and international titles in the slalom and giant slalom disciplines. In 1948, she participated in the Winter Olympics in St. Moritz, Switzerland, where she finished fifth in the slalom.

After her skiing career, Françoise Gignoux turned to aviation and became an experienced pilot. She participated in endurance and speed races, as well as international competitions such as the Gordon Bennett Cup and the Schneider Cup. In 1953, she broke the world record for female speed, reaching a speed of 620 km/h.

In addition to her exceptional achievements in sports, Françoise Gignoux was also a pioneer for women in aviation. She was the first woman to receive a pilot's license in France and worked for aircraft manufacturers such as Dassault and Sud-Aviation.

In 1960, Françoise Gignoux received the Legion of Honor for her exceptional contributions to French sports. She died in 2007 at the age of 77. Her influence and legacy in winter and aviation sports continue to resonate today.
