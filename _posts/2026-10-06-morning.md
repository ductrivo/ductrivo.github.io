### PFAS in eggs: three persistent pollutants detected in Communay, Rhône


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/a57ec309-238d-41ee-a526-1835e6aea017/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="PFAS in eggs: three persistent pollutants detected in Communay, Rhône"
    caption="PFAS in eggs: three persistent pollutants detected in Communay, Rhône" %}


PFAS pollutants have been detected in eggs collected in Communay, located in the Rhône, bordering the Isère. The investigation was conducted by ICI and Radio France's investigative unit, which revealed these results on Tuesday. PFAS, or perfluorochemicals, are persistent and toxic pollutants, widely used in industry for their resistance to grease and water. They have been detected in the eggs of chickens raised on a farm near a chemical plant. Health authorities are currently investigating to determine if this pollution is linked to the plant or other sources.

### The dahlia, this extraordinary flower that deserves to be used more often.


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/0ddebe45-16d0-4014-ae15-124938665821/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="The dahlia, this extraordinary flower that deserves to be used more often."
    caption="The dahlia, this extraordinary flower that deserves to be used more often." %}


The Dahlia is a remarkable flower that deserves more attention, especially in autumn. While chrysanthemums and forget-me-nots are often associated with flowers of this season, the Dahlia is a plant that blooms until the frosts and can provide beautiful cut flowers for bouquets. Originating from Mexico, this cultivar prefers well-drained soil and full sun. There are many varieties of Dahlias, ranging from small to large, brightly colored flowers, offering a great diversity in gardens. Dahlias are also appreciated for their long flowering period and resistance to diseases. They can be grown in pots or directly in the ground, and are typically planted in the spring to bloom in early autumn.

### 'Keeping an eye on my children': When parents get involved in high school students' struggles


Title: Parents Get Involved in School Disruptions

The title "Keeping an Eye on My Children" refers to the growing practice of parents intervening when their children are involved in school disruptions. This trend has been observed in several high schools in France, notably in Paris and Marseille.

Parents are getting involved for various reasons: concerns about their child's safety, solidarity with other parents, or to support the student movement. They sometimes protest by holding banners, singing, or talking to school authorities.

This practice has elicited diverse reactions. Some believe that parents are right to get involved to protect their children, while others argue that it would disrupt the normal school environment and the efforts of teachers to maintain order.

School authorities have also responded to these events. They have sometimes called the police to deal with the protests, but also attempted to negotiate with parents to find a solution acceptable to all.

This situation highlights the importance of the relationship between parents and school authorities, as well as the role of parents in their children's school lives. It is important to find a balance between safety and the normal school environment, and the right of parents to be involved in their child's education.
