### Why is the pig the totem animal of Saint-Antoine-l'Abbaye?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/21951d5a-2865-4ade-b072-9c5a86c90679/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Why is the pig the totem animal of Saint-Antoine-l'Abbaye?"
    caption="Why is the pig the totem animal of Saint-Antoine-l'Abbaye?" %}


The pig is the totem animal of Saint-Antoine-l'Abbaye, due to historical reasons dating back to the Middle Ages. The Order of Saint Anthony, which had its headquarters in the village, had the right to allow its pigs to roam freely in the village streets, equipped with a bell. This practice was linked to the hospital, a care facility for the poor and sick, which was part of the order's activities. The pigs were raised for their meat and fat, which were used to feed the poor and to produce wax for the hospital's needs. The bell on the pigs allowed the villagers to identify and avoid them when they roamed in the streets. This tradition is still alive today in Saint-Antoine-l'Abbaye, where pigs are still raised for the production of wax and to symbolize the medieval hospital.

*[Source](https://www.ici.fr/emissions/tout-s-explique/pourquoi-le-cochon-est-il-l-animal-totem-de-saint-antoine-l-abbaye-3233661?at_medium=lien_RSS&at_locale=29)*
