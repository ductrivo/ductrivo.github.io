### The chameleon disease, a disease 'still too little known,' according to Muriel Salvat, a cardiologist at the Grenoble University Hospital.


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/8225db20-c68f-425b-9c1b-4dafa81b95c2/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="The chameleon disease, a disease 'still too little known,' according to Muriel Salvat, a cardiologist at the Grenoble University Hospital."
    caption="The chameleon disease, a disease 'still too little known,' according to Muriel Salvat, a cardiologist at the Grenoble University Hospital." %}


The chameleon disease, a chronic heart condition that is still relatively unknown, is currently the subject of a national screening campaign being conducted by the Grenoble University Hospital during the winter. This disease can take several years to diagnose and can lead to serious complications if not treated quickly. The chameleon disease is characterized by an abnormal variation in heart rhythm, which can change depending on the person's physical or emotional state. Although relatively unknown, this disease can affect thousands of people and requires rapid screening to allow for effective treatment. The national screening campaign, aimed at raising awareness of the symptoms of the chameleon disease, is being conducted by the Grenoble University Hospital in collaboration with other French medical institutions.

### In the production of wine, why have some local grape varieties been forgotten?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/81b58c74-9983-4585-a42c-e78bcfa68c6a/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="In the production of wine, why have some local grape varieties been forgotten?"
    caption="In the production of wine, why have some local grape varieties been forgotten?" %}


In wine production, certain local grape varieties have been neglected. These include Persan, Verdesse, Joubertin, and Salagnin, as well as several other regional grape varieties. The reasons for this decline are multifaceted.

Firstly, winemakers often favored more productive or disease-resistant varieties to maximize their yields and profits. Indeed, local grape varieties were often less productive and more susceptible to diseases than popular international varieties such as Cabernet Sauvignon or Chardonnay.

Secondly, the standardization of agriculture also contributed to the disappearance of local grape varieties. Winemakers were encouraged to cultivate identical varieties to facilitate trade and comparisons between them. This led to a homogenization of the wine supply, with an emphasis on the most popular varieties rather than local grape varieties.

Finally, the globalization of the wine market also played a role in the decline of local grape varieties. Winemakers faced increased competition from foreign producers and had to adapt their production to meet the demands of international consumers. This led to a decline in the number of local grape varieties cultivated in favor of more popular varieties on the global market.

Today, some winemakers are trying to rediscover and promote local grape varieties in order to diversify the wine supply and preserve the biodiversity of grape varieties. This also helps to promote local culture and support rural economies.

*[Source](https://www.ici.fr/emissions/tout-s-explique-ici-isere/dans-la-production-de-vin-pourquoi-certaines-varietes-de-vignes-locales-ont-elles-ete-oubliees-8965545?at_medium=lien_RSS&at_locale=29)*

### A town hall in Haute-Savoie launches a campaign against artificial intelligence


A town hall in Haute-Savoie has launched a campaign against artificial intelligence (AI). According to the mayor, this decision is motivated by concerns about the protection of citizens' privacy and security. The town hall has asked internet providers to stop using AI in municipal services, particularly for the processing of personal data. Opponents of this decision argue that AI can be useful for improving public services and reducing costs.
