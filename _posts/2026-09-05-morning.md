### What is the origin of the name La Tour-du-Pin?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/6e4c1c72-8cd3-4026-a121-7f6a2b4be386/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="What is the origin of the name La Tour-du-Pin?"
    caption="What is the origin of the name La Tour-du-Pin?" %}


The name of the commune of La Tour-du-Pin, located in the department of Isère, comes from a fortified tower built in the Middle Ages by the Counts of Savoy to protect their territory from invasions. This tower, initially called "Torre Pineta" or "Tour du Pin", was located on a hill overlooking the valley of the Isère and served as a strategic control point.

During the Wars of Religion in the 16th century, the tower was destroyed by Protestant troops. However, the name "Tour-du-Pin" persisted and eventually became the official name of the commune. Today, La Tour-du-Pin is a small town of about 10,000 inhabitants that still retains vestiges of its medieval past, including the Saint-Jean-Baptiste church and the ruins of the fortified tower.

*[Source](https://www.ici.fr/emissions/tout-s-explique-ici-isere/quelle-est-l-origine-du-nom-de-la-tour-du-pin-5072648?at_medium=lien_RSS&at_locale=29)*


### Why are there thin, straw-like fistule-stalactites in the Choranche cave?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/6ed6ebbc-008a-4511-b2b9-98b6c101a0d7/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Why are there thin, straw-like fistule-stalactites in the Choranche cave?"
    caption="Why are there thin, straw-like fistule-stalactites in the Choranche cave?" %}


The Choranche cave has a unique feature: the existence of thin, straw-like fistule stalactites. This phenomenon occurs in a very small number of caves in France and is related to the shape of the cavity.

The Choranche cave, located in the Massif Central, has a particularly complex structure with many narrow vertical fissures. These fissures are so narrow that the stalactites that form in them are also thin and resemble straws.

Fistule stalactites form when water containing calcium carbonate slowly flows through a narrow fissure, gradually depositing limestone. In the Choranche cave, the particular conditions of the cavity favor the formation of thin and narrow stalactites.

This natural rarity attracts thousands of visitors each year, who come to admire these exceptional limestone formations. The Choranche cave is a remarkable example of the beauty and diversity of karst phenomena in France.
