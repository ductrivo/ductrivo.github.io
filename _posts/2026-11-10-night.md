### A battle against the seasons, that of the peach blight.


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/4fa61c4e-34f2-45e9-bc18-229c7ee4ab27/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="A battle against the seasons, that of the peach blight."
    caption="A battle against the seasons, that of the peach blight." %}


Peach blight is a cryptogam disease that affects peach trees during the winter. Although the fruit season is over, this disease can cause significant damage to the trees and their future production. Peach blight is characterized by the appearance of brown spots on the leaves and branches of infected trees. The spores of the disease are transmitted by wind, birds, or humans, and can also survive in the soil for several years.

Farmers and gardeners can take several measures to prevent and treat peach blight. They can use chemicals to kill the spores of the disease, or biological methods such as the use of parasitic fungi to control the spread of the disease. Trees can also be treated with zinc sulfate to prevent infections.

Peach blight can have negative effects on the agricultural economy, as infected trees may produce less or no fruit. Farmers can lose income and potential customers due to the disease. In addition, peach blight can also have negative effects on the environment, as infected trees can serve as reservoirs for the spores of the disease, which can then be transmitted to other neighboring trees.

In conclusion, peach blight is a cryptogam disease that can cause significant damage to peach trees during the winter. Farmers and gardeners can take several measures to prevent and treat the disease, but it can also have negative effects on the agricultural economy and the environment.
