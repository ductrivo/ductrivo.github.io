### How do the typical tuf waterfalls of the Vercors form?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/7b791d67-9bac-42b5-b717-de37c306e50f/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="How do the typical tuf waterfalls of the Vercors form?"
    caption="How do the typical tuf waterfalls of the Vercors form?" %}


The typical tuf waterfalls of the Vercors are formed through a complex geological process. These natural formations, characterized by their rounded shapes and often covered in aquatic vegetation, are abundant in the Vercors region. Their existence is primarily linked to the nature of the volcanic rock that dominates the massif: volcanic tuff.

The process of forming tuff waterfalls takes place in several stages. First, during the eruption of the volcano, tuff is ejected into the air and quickly solidifies. Then, over time, rain erodes the rock and creates natural cavities. Finally, when the hydrostatic pressure becomes too strong, the walls of these cavities collapse, causing the collapse of tuff blocks that move downwards under the force of gravity.

These collapses create tuff waterfalls, which can reach several meters in height and have a variety of shapes. The tuff waterfalls of the Vercors are particularly remarkable for their abundance and diversity. They are an important part of the local biodiversity, providing a privileged habitat for many plant and animal species.

In conclusion, the tuff waterfalls of the Vercors are exceptional natural formations that are formed through a complex geological process linked to the nature of volcanic rock. They are an important part of the local biodiversity and attract many visitors each year.
