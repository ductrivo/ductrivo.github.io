### The plants that we call annuals are often not actually annuals, and here's why.


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/3eb1f2d9-85aa-491c-9d1a-6b8268b362cd/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="The plants that we call annuals are often not actually annuals, and here's why."
    caption="Les plantes que nous appelons bisannuelles ne le sont bien souvent pas en voici la raison" %}


Plants called "biennuals" are not always biennial, despite being cultivated over two civil years. These unique plants are cultivated over two annual periods, but their life cycle is not always clearly defined as biennial. It is important to clarify this situation. Biennial plants need one year to develop and produce flowers, and then another year to reproduce and form seeds. However, some species may have a shorter or longer cycle, which can make them annual or biennial rather than biennial. For example, some types of beets are cultivated as biennuals, but in reality, they have an annual life cycle. Therefore, it is important to check the life cycle of plants before cultivating them to avoid any confusion.
