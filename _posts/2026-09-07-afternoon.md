In 1864, he built the Belledonne, named after a mountain in the Écrins massif in France, which was equipped with an improved air regeneration system. The ship was presented at the 1867 Paris Universal Exposition and impressed visitors with its ability to remain submerged for several hours without the need for an external air source.

Payerne continued to work on his projects until his death in 1876, but his work was not taken up by the French military authorities, who preferred to develop more traditional submarines. However, Payerne left a significant legacy in the field of naval engineering and is considered a pioneer in the development of early submarines equipped with air regeneration systems.

### The secrets of the wines of the Northern Rhône Valley


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/2ee11a9f-08bc-4da9-b8b2-d0fad34c0529/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="The secrets of the wines of the Northern Rhône Valley"
    caption="The secrets of the wines of the Northern Rhône Valley" %}


The wines from the Northern Rhône Valley are renowned for their quality and complexity. Christophe Santos, a sommelier professor at Tain-l'Hermitage, provides us with a better understanding of these wines.

The Northern Rhône Valley is located in the Massif Central in France. It is divided into several appellations contrôlées (AOCs), such as Hermitage, Cornas, Saint-Joseph, and Côte-Rôtie. The wines from these AOCs are primarily made from Syrah, a black grape variety that gives the wines a powerful aroma and spicy flavors.

Wines from Hermitage are considered the most prestigious in the region. They are characterized by their complexity and aging potential. Wines from Cornas are also renowned for their power and robust structure. Wines from Saint-Joseph are lighter and fresher, with aromas of red fruits and spicy flavors. Wines from Côte-Rôtie are a blend of Syrah and Viognier, which gives the wines a floral note and a rounder texture.

The wines from the Northern Rhône Valley are also known for their terroir, which is influenced by the Mediterranean climate and calcareous soils. The vineyards are located on south-facing slopes, which allows the grapes to benefit from optimal sun exposure.

The wines from the Northern Rhône Valley are appreciated for their quality and complexity. They are ideal for accompanying red meat dishes, such as Provençal stews or pepper steaks. Wines from Hermitage and Côte-Rôtie are also considered to be aging wines, which can be kept for several years to develop their complexity and aroma.
