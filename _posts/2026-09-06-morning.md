### A case of dengue reported near Grenoble, what you need to know about this viral disease.


{% include figure.html
    src="https://france3-regions.franceinfo.fr/image/yg3pniCssf799d8laSaEhMG2g1s/930x620/filters:format(webp)/regions/2026/09/04/6a9a86cbb7da9241140127.jpg"
    alt="A case of dengue reported near Grenoble, what you need to know about this viral disease."
    caption="A case of dengue reported near Grenoble, what you need to know about this viral disease." %}


A case of dengue was recently identified in the municipality of Fontaine, near Grenoble (Isère). A mosquito control operation took place on Friday, September 4th in two neighborhoods of the city. Here is information about this viral disease:

Dengue is an infectious disease transmitted by tiger mosquitoes (Aedes albopictus and Aedes aegypti). It is characterized by symptoms such as fever, joint pain, headache, nausea, and abdominal pain. The disease can also be more serious and cause complications such as internal bleeding or bruises under the skin.

The transmission of dengue occurs when mosquitoes bite an infected person and then bite another person. The mosquitoes are active during the day, especially at dawn and dusk. The disease can also be transmitted through blood transfusion or direct contact with the blood of an infected person.

There is no vaccine against dengue, but it is possible to prevent its transmission by eliminating mosquitoes in the environment. Prevention measures include:

* Eliminating stagnant water sources around homes, such as ponds, flower beds, and plastic containers.
* Protecting windows and doors with shutters or mosquito nets.
* Using mosquito repellent when outdoors.
* Wearing appropriate clothing, especially long-sleeved shirts and waterproof shoes.
* Consulting a doctor if dengue symptoms are present.

The mosquito control operation in Fontaine aims to reduce the number of mosquitoes in the area and thus prevent the spread of the disease. Health authorities have also recommended that residents take preventive measures to protect their health.

Indeed, Daphné Michelas was never proven guilty of any murder, and the story of the inn was transformed into a macabre legend by popular tales.
