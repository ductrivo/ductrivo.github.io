### Why did the textile industry develop so strongly in Northern Isère in the 19th century?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/69c42da6-01a0-4c5e-8692-803da0daee2f/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Why did the textile industry develop so strongly in Northern Isère in the 19th century?"
    caption="Why did the textile industry develop so strongly in Northern Isère in the 19th century?" %}


The textile industry experienced significant development in the northern part of Isère in the 19th century due to a combination of environmental, economic, social, and geographical factors. Abundant natural resources such as water and forests allowed for the production of textile fibers and the hydroelectric power needed to manufacture fabrics. Easy access to national markets, thanks to the proximity of major cities like Lyon, also contributed to the development of this industry.

The abundant and inexpensive labor force, primarily composed of poor farmers seeking employment in the cities, also played an important role. Working conditions were often difficult and wages were low, but this did not prevent the textile industry from becoming one of the main employers in Bourgoin-Jallieu and its surroundings between the late 18th century and the late 19th century.

In summary, the environmental, economic, social, and geographical factors all contributed to the rise of the textile industry in northern Isère in the 19th century, making this region one of the main industrial centers of France.

### 'It's a record': The fund for compensating victims of pesticides exceeds 1,000 applications by 2025


The fund for compensating victims of pesticides reached a record number of over 1,000 applications in 2025. This increase can be attributed to greater public awareness of the harmful effects of chemicals and a better understanding of the rights of victims. The applications concern primarily diseases such as cancer, neurological disorders, and chronic allergies, which have been attributed to exposure to pesticides. The fund has already paid out over 50 million euros in compensation to victims since its creation. Authorities are encouraging people exposed to pesticides to apply for compensation, which may explain the increase in the number of applications. The fund continues to receive new applications and is working to resolve each case individually.

### Bent finger and protein clumps: what is this strange chameleon disease that affects all organs?


Chameleon Disease: a rare condition affecting this exotic reptile. Symptoms include curled toes, weight loss, and a general decline in health. The cause of this disease is still unknown, but it affects all of the chameleon's organs, including the nervous, respiratory, digestive, and skin systems. Affected chameleons may also experience problems with temperature regulation and a low resistance to infection. The disease can be diagnosed through a physical examination and blood tests. There is currently no specific treatment for this disease, but palliative care may include rehydration, supplemental feeding, and careful monitoring of the chameleon's body temperature. Chameleon owners should ensure that their animal is kept in optimal conditions to prevent this disease.
