### Alban de Goulaine of the Petit train de la Mure tells us about the history and the latest news for All Saints' Day.


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/1c57613c-a573-4a3a-8026-a7c47cf49d63/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Alban de Goulaine of the Petit train de la Mure tells us about the history and the latest news for All Saints' Day."
    caption="Alban de Goulaine of the Petit train de la Mure tells us about the history and the latest news for All Saints' Day." %}


The Petit Train de la Mure, a famous tourist attraction in the French Alps, resumed operations in 2021 after a long interruption due to the major collapse that occurred in 2010. Alban de Goulaine, the train's director, tells us the story and the new features for All Saints' Day (November 1st) this year.

The Petit Train de la Mure is a narrow-gauge railway that runs through the Mure gorges in Isère. Built between 1907 and 1908, it was closed in 2010 after the collapse of part of the Saint-Nizier tunnel, caused by landslides.

After a long period of reconstruction, the train reopened its doors in July 2021 with a new route and facilities to offer visitors a more comfortable and safe experience. The new route now includes six stations: Saint-Nizier-du-Mouvestre, La Mure, Les Abrets-en-Dauphiné, Saint-Jean-d'Arvey, Saint-Pierre-de-Chartreuse and Villard-de-Lans.

The Petit Train de la Mure is a unique way to discover the beautiful landscapes of the French Alps and their rich cultural heritage. For All Saints' Day, the train offers special excursions with guided tours of the Saint-Nizier-du-Mouvestre church, a remarkable building from the 12th century. Visitors can also enjoy a panoramic view of the Mure gorges from the top of the mountain.

The Petit Train de la Mure is open every weekend and on public holidays, as well as during school holidays. Tickets can be purchased online or at the Saint-Nizier-du-Mouvestre station.

### Why was the CEA established in Grenoble in the late 1950s?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/2558a958-c71a-4a41-ac05-02acf8b44b25/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="Why was the CEA established in Grenoble in the late 1950s?"
    caption="Why was the CEA established in Grenoble in the late 1950s?" %}


The Atomic Research Center (CEA) was established in Grenoble in the 1950s due to a favorable environment and a combination of factors. The city, the capital of the Alps, offered a suitable environment for scientific research thanks to its cool and humid climate, which allowed for the maintenance of cold facilities without requiring high energy consumption. Furthermore, Grenoble already had a significant presence of universities and industries in the field of nuclear technologies, which facilitated access to the skilled workforce needed for the development of the CEA. Finally, Grenoble's location on the border between metropolitan France and Switzerland allowed for optimal nuclear security while also facilitating international exchanges in the field of scientific research. Thus, the combination of these factors contributed to choosing Grenoble to host the CEA.
