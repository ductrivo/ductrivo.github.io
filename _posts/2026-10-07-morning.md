### How was the Vercors rock-cut road in the Bourne gorge built?


{% include figure.html
    src="https://www.ici.fr/pikapi/images/b4822b87-7983-46b5-9f2e-0a33845ea203/920x517?webp=false"
    alt="How was the Vercors rock-cut road in the Bourne gorge built?"
    caption="How was the Vercors rock-cut road in the Bourne gorge built?" %}


The road with retaining walls in the Vercors gorges, along the Bourne river, was built between 1863 and 1872. It connects Pont-en-Royans to Villard-de-Lans and was opened in 1872, after a decade of hard work. The road is characterized by suspension bridges and retaining walls in the steep rock faces of the gorges. It was built to facilitate traffic between the towns of Vercors, but its construction was a major technical challenge due to rock erosion and difficult weather conditions. The work required the use of hundreds of workers and cost more than 2 million francs at the time. The Vercors retaining wall road is now classified as a UNESCO World Heritage site as part of the Mont-Cenis and Alps railway.
